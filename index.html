Giardini

Day one, 28 October. The national pavilions in the gardens, plus the Central Pavilion of the main exhibition.

Spain

Oriol Vilanova

Spanish Pavilion

Vilanova collects postcards from flea markets, and here he papers the pavilion walls edge to edge with about 20,000 of them. From a distance they read as fields of colour. Up close they range from classical sculpture and Alpine peaks to kitsch holiday views.

Kenya

Wangechi Mutu

MothersMound, 2026

Central Pavilion · In Minor Keys

A rounded earthen mound shaped like a pregnant body anchors a room of Mutu's work. A sunset landscape and drifting lines of text are projected onto the curtains around it. It belongs to her retelling of Eden, in which Mother Earth takes the place of God the Father.

1 item

Hungary

Endre Koronczi

Pneuma Cosmic

Hungarian Pavilion

In Both Feet Above the Ground, Koronczi walked for a year in search of “the most important sigh.” He captured it in a blown-glass vessel and carried it, sealed, on his back. Stacked screens follow the pilgrimage through forest, snow and city streets at night.

USA

Alma Allen

Call Me the Breeze

United States Pavilion

Every one of Allen's biomorphic sculptures in polished bronze and stone is called Not Yet Titled. In the rotunda, a tall ribbed bronze twists upward like something still growing. Curator Jeffrey Uslip frames the show as art that embodies “a state of alterity, weightlessness, and freedom of thought.”

2 items

Nordic Pavilion · Norway

Tori Wrånes

Human Lace

Nordic Pavilion · How Many Angels Can Dance on the Head of a Pin?

Towering, headless figures in padded cream workwear, with cast-bronze hands, crawl, arch and interlock through Sverre Fehn's concrete pavilion. They look caught mid-metamorphosis, on their way to becoming birds.

Nordic Pavilion · Sweden

Klara Kristalova

Lust for Life

Nordic Pavilion · How Many Angels Can Dance on the Head of a Pin?

A monumental fallen tree is also a reclining woman, with her pale face and long black hair at one end. Her trunk is home to Kristalova's hybrid creatures in glazed ceramic, bronze and wood: a fox, crows and small forest spirits.

3 items

Japan

Ei Arakawa-Nash

Grass Babies, Moon Babies

Japanese Pavilion

208 weighted baby dolls in reflective sunglasses climb scaffolding, cling to the stair rail and gather under a canopy of hanging feeding bottles. Visitors can adopt a doll and carry it for the length of their visit while a soundscape made from the artist's twins plays. It's a playful look at care work and Japan's falling birth rate.

3 items

Germany

Sung Tieu

German Pavilion · with Henrike Naumann

Tieu clad the pavilion's façade in a 1:1 trompe-l'œil mosaic of an East Berlin housing block, graffiti included, made from more than three million marble tesserae. Inside, Henrike Naumann's mint-green rooms of halved chairs and flattened furniture continue the pavilion's look at German reunification.

Arsenale: In Minor Keys

Day two, 29 October. The main exhibition, conceived by the late curator Koyo Kouoh, runs the length of the Corderie (the Arsenale's old rope works) and out into the Giardino delle Vergini.

Australia

Khaled Sabsabi

khalil, 2026

Corderie · Video

A canvas about 40 metres long curves into a circle around you. Shapes and colours are projected across it, drifting, merging and splitting apart to repetitive music. Sabsabi was born in Lebanon, lives in Sydney, and also represents Australia in the Giardini.

2 items

South Africa

Nicholas Hlobo

Corderie

Trumpets, horns and a saxophone sprawl across a low platform, joined by long looping tubes. Hlobo's “orchestras” of brass instruments are built to sound without a player.

1 item

El Salvador

Guadalupe Maravilla

ICE Age Disease Thrower

Corderie

Woven, body-like chairs, which echo Central American Acapulco chairs and Mayan hammocks, hold objects Maravilla gathered along his childhood migration route to the US. His “disease throwers” are healing machines. One holds a child's beanie with animal ears, modelled on one worn by a child in US immigration detention.

USA

Carrie Schneider

First Living Woman

Corderie

A chain of curved photographic prints, roughly a kilometre long, repeats the eyes of actress Hélène Chatelain from eight seconds of Chris Marker's La Jetée. As you walk past, the eyes seem to follow you.

USA

Nick Cave

Two Points in Time – At Once

Corderie, harbour and Giardino delle Vergini

Bronzes cast from Cave's own body are staged as a seven-station procession through the stages of grief. A seated figure sprouts branches and birds where its head should be. Siren stacks bronze arms into a totem that sprouts gramophone horns, and another figure rushes forward under a mass of flowers. Outside, Amalgam (Origin) towers over the harbour, its chest turning into a tree for about 50 bird species. In the garden, a cross-legged figure sits beneath a great floral halo.

Canada

Rajni Perera & Marigold Santos

Efflorescence / The Way We Wake, 2023

Corderie

A masked deity made of polymer clay, Styrofoam and synthetic hair rises among terracotta “atolls” that sprout strange, spiky flowers. The Canada-based duo, born in Sri Lanka and the Philippines, build hybrid, diasporic mythologies.

Cameroon

Werewere Liking

Corderie

A painting by the Cameroonian-born writer, painter and theatre-maker, who founded the Ki-Yi village arts community in Abidjan. It hangs on a brick-clad wall beside her text Sous l'Égide des Pyramides (2023), a pan-African invocation of Kemet.

1 item

France

Kader Attia

Whisper of Traces

Corderie · Video

Ropes hung with shards of broken mirror and dried plants throw scattered light across projected films. Attia starts from a Vietnamese shaman's claim that computer viruses are spirits, and asks why we credit AI with near-religious powers.

Attribution likely

2 items

India

Himali Singh Soin & David Soin Tappeser

Corderie

A forest of hanging tapestries in blue, white and terracotta. They are woven from ahimsa silk, and their patterns transcribe sound waves. The writer-artist and the percussionist make a space for listening that links the Indian subcontinent and Antarctica across deep time.

1 item

Chile

Alfredo Jaar

The End of the World, 2023–24

Corderie

A corridor flooded with red light leads to a single small cube. On the wall, texts trace the minerals behind the energy transition (cobalt, lithium, rare earths, copper, nickel) and the wars, extraction and inequality they feed.

4 items

South Africa

Kemang Wa Lehulere

I Bleach My Words for Your Comfort

Arsenale

An “allegorical library.” Green shelves hold more than 700 clay paving bricks painted chalkboard-black and shelved like books. Cast hands forming letters of the sign-language alphabet are scattered among them. The work is about censorship and the violence of controlled knowledge.

2 items

USA

Laurie Anderson

Notebook

Arsenale

A black room covered in Anderson's white drawings and handwriting, with sound throughout. There are dogs and crocodiles, a line from her teacher Mingyur Rinpoche (“Try to practice how to feel sad without actually being sad”), and the sign-off “I had a lot more to say but I ran out of paint.”

2 items

Switzerland

Uriel Orlow

Botanical Biennale, 2026

Giardino delle Vergini

Silkscreen billboards map where the plants growing around the Biennale's sites first came from. It is part of Orlow's long-running work on botany, migration and history.

Arsenale: National Pavilions

The countries showing in the Corderie, the Artiglierie, the Sale d'Armi, the Isolotto and the Tese.

Lebanon

Nabil Nahas

Don't Get Me Wrong

Lebanese Pavilion · Corderie

A 45-metre frieze of 26 acrylic canvases is hung above eye level, “a very Renaissance way of displaying art,” as Nahas puts it. It moves between geometric abstraction and landscapes of cedar, olive and palm.

Attribution likely

1 item

Luxembourg

Aline Bouvy

La Merde

Luxembourg Pavilion · Sale d'Armi

A filmed manifesto stars an anthropomorphic piece of excrement, appearing as a puppet, an animation and a trace. It's screened inside a semi-circular structure of steel and mirrored glass that multiplies your reflection.

2 items

Morocco

Amina Agueznay

Asǝṭṭa

Moroccan Pavilion · Artiglierie

Morocco's first pavilion at the Arsenale. About 150 panels of wool, raffia and fabric, made with 160 artisans, cascade through the hall like a “second skin.” They draw on Amazigh weaving, and a bed of white wool invites visitors to lie down.

2 items

India

Ranjani Shettar

Under the same sky

Indian Pavilion · Geographies of Distance: Remembering Home

Hand-formed flowers, seed pods and leaves in pale and amber tones float through the upper volume of India's pavilion, its first at the Biennale in seven years. Behind them, Asim Waqif's bamboo scaffold Chaal cuts through the space.

8 items

India

Sumakshi Singh

Permanent Address

Indian Pavilion · Geographies of Distance: Remembering Home

A full-scale house rendered entirely in embroidered thread, with walls, leaded windows, a spiral stair and wrought-iron gates. It recreates the artist's demolished family home, and you can walk through its openings.

3 items

Uzbekistan

The Aural Sea

Uzbekistan Pavilion · group show

An imaginative requiem for the vanished Aral Sea. Aygul Sarsen, from Karakalpakstan, paints the sea as a feminine deity, as in Kutiw (Waiting) and her sketch series. Xin Liu's hand-woven plastic slowly dissolves in tanks of enzyme solution over the Biennale's run. A.A. Murakami's The Sun Sets in a Shell is a vast tapestry coded from zebra-mussel patterns. The glowing orange painting is probably Jahongir Bobokulov's Aura.

5 items

China

Nie Shichang

Kinetic Droplet Instrument (Di Dong Yi)

China Pavilion · Dream Stream · Video

A mechanical sculpture of nested metal rings recreates the ripples spreading out from a falling water drop. Behind it is Wang Dongling's giant cursive calligraphy, co-written with a robot.

Castello

A pavilion in a former shipyard, a short walk from the Arsenale.

Iceland

Ásta Fanney Sigurðardóttir

Pocket Universe

Icelandic Pavilion · former shipyard, Castello · Video

A string of rooms in an old shipyard between the Giardini and the Arsenale, painted white and sky blue. A surreal creation-story film features the artist as “Creature Zero.” Around it are glowing orbs, talismans, handwritten poems and a sculpture made of Icelandic wool.
